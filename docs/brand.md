# Mzansi Money Matters (MMM): voice guide for drafts

**What MMM covers:** the broad economy, finance and personal finance, mostly South
African with an international angle. Above all: **new laws and policy decisions, and
how they hit South African households and the economy.**

**Who it's for:** ordinary South African households and working people who want to
understand what budgets, interest rates, fuel prices and new laws mean for their pocket.

## How MMM sounds
- Plain language. Explain jargon the first time ("repo rate: the rate the Reserve Bank charges banks").
- Always answer **"what does this mean for my household?"** with a rand example where possible.
- Calm and factual, not alarmist. No hype, no "get rich" promises.
- Local: rand, SARS, SARB, Stats SA, petrol inland/coast, taxi fares, bond, store cards.
- End with a question or a call to follow, to get comments.

## Rules every post must follow
1. **Every number has a source** in the `sources` column (official first: Treasury, SARB, Stats SA, Government Gazette, DMPR; then news).
2. **Forecasts are labelled as forecasts.** Proposals and bills are not called laws until signed.
3. Personal-finance tips end with: *General information, not financial advice.* MMM is not a
   registered financial services provider, so posts educate; they never tell a specific person what to buy.
4. No naming or shaming private individuals.
5. Hashtags: `#MzansiMoneyMatters` plus 1–3 topic tags.

## Formats that work
- "X in 60 seconds": a big announcement in 4–5 bullets.
- "What it means for your bond / car / groceries": one decision, one rand example.
- "Why a war far away hits your petrol tank": international → local chain.
- "3 moves to make this month": practical, general tips.
